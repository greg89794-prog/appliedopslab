Where Optimization Meets the Shop Floor

Primary focus

Cutting-Stock Optimization

Exploring more efficient cutting patterns for metal tubing, bar stock, aluminum extrusions, and other linear materials, accounting for stock lengths, saw kerf, reusable remnants, and multiple customer orders.

Related areas

Secondary focus

Material Waste & Remnant Reuse

Investigating how shops manage leftover material and whether practical optimization could improve material utilization beyond existing workflows.

Exploratory

Production Scheduling

Exploring how shops coordinate machine availability, setup times, urgent jobs, and delivery deadlines when planning becomes difficult.

About the Researcher

I’m Greg, the independent researcher behind Applied Ops Lab, based in Morris County, New Jersey.

I’m interested in how mathematical optimization can address practical manufacturing problems—particularly cutting plans, reusable material, and production constraints.

Right now, I’m learning from local fabrication shops about the planning challenges that existing software doesn’t fully address.

Does Your Shop Still Adjust Cutting Plans Manually?

I’m speaking with local fabrication professionals to understand how they handle multiple orders, different stock lengths, and reusable offcuts.

Does your cutting software handle these situations well, or are there still cases where someone on the shop floor finds a better arrangement?

I’d welcome a brief exchange—no sales pitch, just a practical conversation about what works and what doesn’t.
